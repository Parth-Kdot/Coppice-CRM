# How the 8 Coppice directions were chosen

Written 2026-10-27 during the overnight design run.

## The starting field

I wrote up 13 candidate directions, each with a layout paradigm, palette, type pairing, icon and logo idea, a landing animation idea and an in-app motion idea: Ledger, Graphite, Ring Count, Transit, Stamp, Dusk, Blueprint, Trailhead, The Record, Riso Zine, Aurora Midnight, Bento Clubhouse and Rolodex. Then four independent critics each scored all 13 from one lens only. The first lens was how a sponsorship chair, a hackathon team, an exec board at another school and a nonprofit alumni board would react, including when the screen is shared. The second was professionalism, credibility and trend risk. The third was functionality, density, contrast and whether a metaphor gets in the way of claiming, logging and following up. The fourth was coolness, motion potential and how different each one is from the rest. A fifth critic then argued against whatever set came out on top.

## What got cut and why

Aurora Midnight went first. Every critic read it as the generic dark "AI startup" look, and it added nothing Ring Count did not already do better with a story behind it. Bento Clubhouse turned out to be a softer copy of Stamp with the conviction taken out. Rolodex scored 2 on function because flipping one card at a time is a slow way to scan 40 companies, and its paper and red ruling sat too close to Ledger. Riso Zine was the hardest of the four to drop, since its landing page could be beautiful, but print grain and overprinted color fight legibility in a tool people read for hours, and it shared its hand-stamped, multicolor energy with Stamp.

The real argument was over the last slot. By raw score the eighth place belonged to Trailhead. The contrarian made the case that Trailhead and Transit are the same idea twice: both put the six stages in a strip across the top as stops on a route, both use typefaces descended from road signage, and both animate a marker moving along that line. Since you asked for directions that share almost nothing, two slots spent on one navigation idea is a poor trade. Trailhead's summit metaphor also says nothing about coppicing. The Record scored two points lower, which with four critics is noise, and it brings things nobody else in the set has: a masthead with a dateline as navigation, the queue presented as today's edition, company pages written up as profiles, and the most natural home for the Turnover Time Capsule as a year-in-review issue handed to the next board. The risk with The Record is drifting into Ledger, so the two are now pulled apart on purpose. Ledger keeps warm cream, moss and a book serif; The Record gets cool newsprint gray, pure black, one red, and a high-contrast headline serif with a Franklin-style news sans.

The second change came from the range check. Seven of the eight leading candidates were light. Ring Count was the only dark direction, which leaves anyone who works at night or shares a dim screen with a single option. Dusk already had the right name for it, so Dusk is now dark-first in plum and apricot. That gives two dark options with opposite moods: Ring Count warm bark and amber, Dusk cool plum.

## The final eight

1. **Ledger.** Warm, light and story-led. A bound-ledger spine with thumb-index tabs replaces a plain sidebar, tables are ruled, and every table carries a small board-year margin with a double rule where one board's entries end and the next one's begin, so the "new board, same roots" idea shows up during an ordinary screen-share.
2. **Graphite.** The neutral power tool. Icon rail, dense list and an inspector pane, with the command palette at the center. It sets the bar for speed and density that the others are judged against.
3. **Transit.** Swiss signage clarity, and the only direction where the pipeline itself is the navigation: a line strip of six numbered stations runs across every screen, and the queue is a departures board. Overdue is the only red.
4. **Ring Count.** Warm dark-first. Growth rings carry history and claim expiry, while real statistics stay as plain bars so the data never becomes a puzzle.
5. **Blueprint.** Technical drawing. A floating tool dock instead of a sidebar or top bar, content on white drawing sheets over a faint grid, and each company page carries an engineering title block.
6. **Stamp.** The loud, playful one, built as a bento grid where the overdue queue is always the biggest block. Thick borders, hard shadows, and claiming a company stamps it.
7. **Dusk.** Calm and dark. One company at a time in a focus view, with a list toggle for scanning, and a bottom action bar instead of a sidebar.
8. **The Record.** Editorial. Masthead and dateline on top, the queue as today's edition with the most overdue company as the lead story and the rest as a dense table, company pages as profiles.

## Fixes the critics asked for, adopted in the briefs

Each brief includes the critics' fixes for that direction. Ledger drops the soft, wonky Fraunces cliché for a book serif, uses plain icons under 24 px (hatching only at large sizes and on the landing), darkens overdue to pass contrast, and adds keyboard shortcuts. Transit picks six station colors that avoid red and orange and survive color-blindness simulation, gives every stage a number and short code so color is never the only signal, keeps split-flap motion off in-app lists (counters only, under 200 ms, off with reduced motion), replaces the subway-bullet logo with the six-stop line bending into a C, adds an information-desk drawer for the future Copilot, and shows a "service change" banner when a new board takes over. Ring Count uses its display face's tabular figures instead of a monospace. Stamp makes the queue the largest tile and limits itself to two inks plus black so it stays credible. Blueprint keeps the grid faint behind white sheets so contrast never suffers.

Two overlaps across the whole set were also fixed. Three logos were all tree stumps, so only Ledger keeps the coppice stool; Ring Count uses concentric growth arcs, and Blueprint uses a section-cut marker that forms a C. Four landing pages leaned on the same trick of a line drawing itself, so each direction now has its own signature move: stems growing for Ledger, a split-flap board and a train of company cards for Transit, an exploded drawing that assembles for Blueprint, rings spreading on canvas for Ring Count, typesetting and a press run for The Record, stickers slapping down for Stamp, a scripted cursor demo for Graphite, and a horizon light shift with a triaging card stack for Dusk.
