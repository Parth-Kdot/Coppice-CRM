## 2. The problem

### Headline

Five ways a shared spreadsheet lets your team down

### Subhead

A sheet with an owner column and a few cell colors holds up for the first weeks of outreach. Somewhere around the time the third officer starts adding rows, these five things begin to happen.

### Items (each pairs the problem with what Coppice does about it)

**1. Two officers pitch the same company**
The owner cell was empty, or out of date, or filled in a minute after the other officer pressed Send. The sponsor receives two introductions from one club, sometimes with different asks.
*What Coppice does:* You claim a company before you can log outreach on it. The database lets exactly one officer hold it, and everyone else sees that officer's name and the date the claim ends.

**2. Follow-ups slip**
Nothing in a sheet says which rows are due today. The reply that needed an answer on the 9th is still sitting in row 84 on the 20th, and the only reminder is whoever happens to scroll past it.
*What Coppice does:* Each outreach entry carries a follow-up date, set to 7 days out unless you change it. The home screen lists every company that is due, most overdue first.

**3. Nobody knows which pitch works**
Three versions of the intro email went out this semester. Replies came back to six different inboxes, and the one "Replied?" column was last updated in October.
*What Coppice does:* Every outreach entry already records its channel, its date and who sent it, and when you send one of Coppice's drafts the entry can record which draft it was. Reply rate per pitch template is the next feature being built, so that comparison will sit in one place.

**4. Duplicate rows**
"Acme Corp" is in row 12 and "acme corp" is in row 140. Two more rows list acme.com and www.acme.com. Each one has a different officer's name next to it.
*What Coppice does:* It flags look-alike names and websites and shows them together. It never merges or deletes anything. A person looks and decides.

**5. History leaves with the graduating board**
The context lived in a senior's head and in a Drive folder that gets cleaned out in May. In August the new chair inherits a list of company names and has to guess who was contacted, who said no, and why.
*What Coppice does:* Notes, channels, dates and stage changes sit on each company, inside a workspace that outlives its officers.

### Closing line for the section

You can import last year's sheet as it is, columns and all, and sort out the rest as you go.

## 3. How it works

### Headline

From last year's sheet to a pipeline the whole team can work from

### Subhead

The first step is done once when the workspace is set up, and the other four become part of an officer's week.

### Steps

**Step 1. Import last year's sheet** (Owner or Officer)
Export your spreadsheet as a CSV and upload it, up to 2,000 rows. Point each column at the right field, then look over any groups of look-alike companies and choose, group by group, whether to skip them or import them. You can also add companies one at a time.

**Step 2. Claim a company before you contact it** (Officer)
Press Claim on any company nobody else holds. It is yours for your team's claim window, which is 14 days unless your chair changed it. Everyone else sees your name and the date the claim ends.

**Step 3. Contact from your own inbox, then log it** (Officer)
Write the email yourself, or open a draft in Gmail or Outlook from Coppice, and send it from your own account. Back in Coppice, record the channel (email, LinkedIn, in person or other), add a note, and choose a follow-up date. The date starts at 7 days from today. Saving the entry renews your claim.

**Step 4. The queue brings it back** (Officer)
On the follow-up date the company appears on your home screen, with the most overdue ones first and your last note beside each name. As things happen, move it along the board: Replied, In conversation, Committed or Declined.

**Step 5. The next board inherits the story** (Owner and Officer)
When officers graduate, the workspace stays. The incoming chair opens any company, reads every outreach entry, note and stage change in order, claims it, and picks up from there.

### Numbers strip (optional, each tied to a real default or limit)

- **14 days:** default claim window (owners can set 1 to 60)
- **7 days:** default time to the next follow-up
- **6 stages:** Not contacted, Contacted, Replied, In conversation, Committed, Declined
- **2,000 rows:** per CSV import

## 4. Features

### Headline

What is in the workspace today

### Subhead

Each of these exists in the app now, except the block labeled Coming next.

### Feature blocks

**1. Claim before you contact**
*Short:* One officer holds a company at a time, and the database enforces it.
*Long:* When two officers press Claim in the same second, one claim goes through and the other officer is told who got there first and until when. While a claim is live, nobody else can log outreach on that company, change its stage or archive it. The claim ends after your team's claim window and renews each time you log outreach. Owners can force-release a claim from an officer who went quiet, and a claim is released automatically when its holder leaves the workspace. Expiry is judged by the database's clock, so a laptop with the wrong time cannot hold a company longer than it should.

**2. A follow-up queue as the home screen**
*Short:* Every company that is due, most overdue first.
*Long:* The first thing you see after signing in is the list of open companies whose follow-up date has arrived, with how many days overdue each one is and your last note beside it. An "Only mine" filter narrows the list to companies you currently hold. A second list shows companies nobody has contacted yet, leaving out the ones another officer has claimed. Companies marked Committed or Declined leave the queue.

**3. A stage board**
*Short:* Six stages from first contact to a decision.
*Long:* Not contacted, Contacted, Replied, In conversation, Committed and Declined. A company moves from Not contacted to Contacted on its own when you log the first outreach, and every later move is recorded with who made it. Moving a company to Committed or Declined closes the pitch and clears its claim, and Coppice asks you to confirm first.

**4. An outreach log on every company**
*Short:* Emails, LinkedIn messages, conversations and notes, all on the company.
*Long:* Each entry shows the date, who logged it, the channel (Email, LinkedIn, In person or Other) and what was said, plus the next follow-up date. The company page also keeps a stage history. When the person who knew the contact has graduated, the record is still there to read.

**5. Duplicates are flagged, never merged**
*Short:* Coppice points at look-alikes. A person decides.
*Long:* Names are compared with capital letters, punctuation, a leading "the" and endings like Inc, LLC and Corp ignored, so "Acme, Inc.", "ACME Corp" and "acme corporation" count as the same name. Websites are compared without "www" or the path, so acme.com and www.acme.com match. Matching companies are grouped and shown together. Nothing is merged, blocked or removed on its own.

**6. CSV import**
*Short:* Bring last year's sheet in, up to 2,000 rows.
*Long:* Upload a CSV, map your columns to Coppice's fields, and review the result before anything is saved. Groups of look-alike companies are listed together, and for each group you choose whether to skip it or import it. Files can be up to 3 MB.

**7. Pitch drafts that stick to verified facts** (AI, optional)
*Short:* Drafts built only from facts your owner has checked.
*Long:* The owner uploads the sponsorship packet and Coppice lists the facts in it, each with the page number and the quote it came from. A fact can be used in a draft only after the owner verifies it. Each request produces two variants. Numbers that no verified fact supports, links, and lines like "thanks for sponsoring us before" are underlined so you can check them before sending. When a draft is ready, it opens in your own Gmail or Outlook compose window and you press Send from your own address. Owners can switch AI off for the whole workspace.

**8. History that survives turnover**
*Short:* The workspace outlives the board.
*Long:* Companies, outreach entries, notes and stage changes belong to the workspace, whoever typed them. When an officer leaves, their claims are released and everything they logged stays. Archiving a company hides it from the board and queue and keeps its history.

## 5. Live example

### Headline

Maya and Dev, same company, same second

### Subhead

A made-up club, a real race. Here is what each officer sees when they both go for the same lead on Monday morning, and what brings it back two weeks later.

### Setting (for the designers, and as a caption)

Alder State Rocketry Club is a fictional student org with a 14-day claim window. The company is Kestrel Aerospace, imported from last year's sheet and never contacted. Maya and Dev are officers. Sam, the chair, is an Owner and appears only in the alternate ending.

### Script (six beats)

**Beat 1. Monday, Oct 26, 9:02 AM. Two screens, one lead.**
Maya and Dev open the home screen a few seconds apart. Kestrel Aerospace is in the "Never contacted" list on both, with a Claim button next to it.
*Caption:* Two officers, one promising lead, and both about to click.

**Beat 2. 9:02 AM. Both press Claim.**
The database accepts whichever request reaches it first. Maya's screen reads: "You hold this until Nov 9, 2026, 9:02 AM." Dev's screen reads: "Maya claimed this first. They hold it until Nov 9, 2026, 9:02 AM."
*Caption:* One winner. The other officer is told who and until when.

**Beat 3. 9:03 AM. Dev moves on.**
Dev opens Kestrel's page and finds the claim box reading "Held by Maya until Nov 9." There is no outreach form for him, and the stage can only be changed by Maya or an owner. He goes back to the queue and claims Birchwood Foods, the next company down.
*Caption:* Nobody had to ask in the group chat.

**Beat 4. Wednesday, Oct 28. Maya sends and logs.**
Maya writes to Priya in partnerships from her own Gmail. In Coppice she logs the outreach: channel Email, note "Intro to Priya in partnerships, packet attached," follow-up on Nov 4, which is the 7-day default. Kestrel moves from Not contacted to Contacted, and her claim renews to Nov 11.
*Caption:* Logging the email renews the claim for another 14 days.

**Beat 5. Wednesday, Nov 4. The follow-up comes back.**
Maya's due list shows Kestrel Aerospace marked "Due today," with her note from the 28th under the name. Dev's queue shows it too, labeled "Held by Maya until Nov 11," with no Claim button. Maya sends a short follow-up from her inbox and logs it, with the next follow-up set for Nov 11. Her claim now runs to Nov 18.
*Caption:* The queue remembers so Maya does not have to.

**Beat 6. Friday, Nov 6. A reply, and a record.**
Priya answers. Maya logs it as an Other entry with the note "Priya replied, wants a 20-minute call," sets the next follow-up date, and moves Kestrel to Replied. Next September, the new chair opens Kestrel Aerospace and reads the page top to bottom: an email on Oct 28 and a follow-up on Nov 4, both from Maya, a note on Nov 6, and a stage history that goes Not contacted, Contacted, Replied.
*Caption:* The new board starts from Maya's notes.

### Alternate ending (optional seventh beat, for a version that shows expiry)

Suppose Maya had logged nothing after Oct 28. On Nov 11 her claim runs out. Dev's page for Kestrel reads "Maya's claim expired Nov 11, 2026," with a Claim button. If Sam, the chair, had wanted it freed sooner, the Force release button would have asked "Release Maya's claim on Kestrel Aerospace?" before clearing it.

### Cues for animation (suggestions only)

A split screen with two cursors lands on the same Claim button. Dev's button turns into the "claimed this first" message while Maya's turns into "You hold this." A thin date strip from Oct 26 to Nov 18 shows the claim bar extending each time Maya logs, and a small marker on Nov 4 pulls Kestrel back to the top of the queue.

## 7. The name

### Headline

Many stems, one root

### Subhead

Coppice is said KOP-iss. The first syllable sounds like "cop."

### Body

Coppicing is an old woodland practice. A woodcutter fells a tree down to its stump on a regular cycle, and the stump answers by sending up a crop of new stems from the same roots. Some coppiced stumps have been cut and regrown for centuries.

A student board works on a similar rhythm. Every spring the officers graduate, and what remains is the set of relationships with companies: who was asked, who said yes, who said to try again in the fall. Coppice keeps those in the workspace so the next board grows back from them.

The name has a second reading in its first syllables, co-piece: working alongside someone, each of you holding a piece of the same list.

### Short lines to pull out

- Many stems, one root.

## 8. Trust and boundaries

### Headline

What Coppice will never do

### Subhead

These are limits built into how the app works, and each one has a specific mechanism behind it.

### Items

**Send email for you**
Coppice has no send button. A draft opens in your own Gmail or Outlook compose window, and you press Send from your own address. Coppice never connects to your inbox.

**Merge your data silently**
Look-alike companies are flagged and shown together. Nothing is merged, blocked or deleted until a person decides what to do with the group.

**Let AI write to your pipeline without a person confirming**
AI drafts text and lists facts from a packet. A fact cannot be used in a draft until the owner verifies it, a draft stays on the screen until you choose to send it from your own inbox, and a stage, a claim or a log entry changes only when an officer presses the button.

**Share one team's data with another**
Every workspace is separated inside the database, using row-level security in Postgres, so one team's companies, notes and members are not visible to another team's accounts.

**Also out of scope:** payments, and public pages for companies to visit.

### Privacy notes

- **AI is optional.** The owner turns it on or off for the whole workspace and sets how many AI calls the workspace may make in a 30-day period. With AI off, none of the text below is sent anywhere.
- **People's names and email addresses never go to the AI provider.** The provider's free tier may use what it receives to improve its products, so a draft is written with placeholders for first name, sender name and organization name, and Coppice fills in the real names after the draft comes back.
- **What does go to the provider while AI is on:** the text of the packet you uploaded, your verified facts, the company's name, industry and website, the contact's job title, and any note you add to a draft request. Keep anything private out of the packet.
- **Officers do not see other members' email addresses.** Only owners can view them.
- **Company contacts never log in** and never receive anything from Coppice.
- **Archiving keeps the history.** An archived company leaves the board and queue, and it can be unarchived later.

## 9. Get started

### Headline

Set up a workspace in four steps

### Subhead

You need a Google account and last year's sheet, if you have one.

### Steps

**1. Sign in with Google**
One click, with no new password to remember.

**2. Create a workspace, or open your chair's invite link**
The chair creates the workspace and becomes its Owner. Everyone else joins by opening the invite link they were sent.

**3. Invite your officers and set your claim window**
Owners invite officers with a copyable invite link and choose how long a claim lasts, from 1 to 60 days. The default is 14.

**4. Import your sheet and start claiming**
Upload last year's CSV, review any duplicate groups, then press Claim on the first company you plan to contact.

### FAQ

**Is Coppice free?**
Yes for student teams, with no limit on how many officers you add. (Owner to confirm what to say about nonprofits and alumni boards; see the end of this file.)

**Does Coppice send email?**
No. Drafts open in your own Gmail or Outlook, and you send them from your own address. Coppice only records that you reached out, when you tell it.

**What happens when a claim expires?**
At the end of your team's claim window, the company shows who held it and that the claim expired, and any officer can claim it. Logging outreach before then renews the claim for another full window. An owner can also force-release a claim earlier, and a claim is released if its holder leaves the workspace.

**Can two people claim the same company at once?**
No. If two officers press Claim in the same moment, the database lets one through. The other sees who got there first and the date their claim ends.

**What about Microsoft or Outlook accounts?**
Sign-in is with Google today. Your outreach can still come from any inbox: drafts open in Gmail, in Outlook on the web with a work or school Microsoft 365 account, or in your computer's default mail app. (Owner to confirm wording; see the end of this file.)

**Can we bring last year's sheet?**
Yes. Export it as a CSV and upload it, up to 2,000 rows and 3 MB. You map the columns, and any groups of look-alike companies are shown so you can skip or import each group.

**What happens at board turnover?**
The workspace and everything in it stays. The outgoing chair makes the incoming chair an Owner in Settings, then steps down to Officer or leaves, and the new board can open any company to read its outreach entries, notes and stage changes. Claims held by someone who leaves the workspace are released.

**Does it work on phones in a browser?**
Yes. Coppice is a website, so there is nothing to install, and you can check the queue, claim a company and log outreach from your phone's browser.

### Optional extra questions

**How is this different from HubSpot's free CRM?**
HubSpot's free tier limits you to 2 users, its owner field has no lock or expiry, and per-template reply stats are on a paid tier. Coppice is free for student teams with unlimited officers, locks each company to one officer for a set window, and is adding reply rate per pitch template. (Owner to re-check these HubSpot details before publishing.)

**Who can do what?**
Owners create the workspace, invite officers, set the claim window, verify the facts AI drafts may use, and can force-release claims. Officers add or import companies, claim them, log outreach and work the queue.

**Do the companies we contact need accounts?**
No. Company contacts never log in, and Coppice never emails them.
